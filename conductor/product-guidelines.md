# Product Guidelines: Korean Localization

## Prose Style
- **Plain (Haera-che):** All written content (lecture notes, exercises) should use the plain, direct, and concise style. This is typical for technical manuals and documentation, providing a clear and authoritative tone.

## Technical Terminology
- **English Focus:** Prioritize the original English technical terms. While Korean explanations are necessary, the primary goal is to ensure students are familiar with the global standard terminology used in the CS industry and academia. Use the English term directly or include it prominently.

## Content Structure
- **Mirror Original:** The Korean version must maintain the exact structure, layout, and flow of the original MIT content. This ensures a consistent experience for students who may refer to both the English and Korean materials.

## Dubbing Principles
- **Natural/Engaging:** The Korean dubbing should be conversational and engaging, similar to a local university lecture. While maintaining technical accuracy, the delivery should be accessible and keep students' interest, rather than being a strictly literal or formal voiceover.

## Visual and Formatting Standards
- **Markdown Consistency:** Follow the existing Markdown formatting used in the project (Kramdown/GFM).
- **Code Blocks:** Keep all code examples and shell commands exactly as they are in the original English version.
